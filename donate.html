Why We Don't Accept Donations

We are developers, not accountants. Our mission is to build software — not to manage financial overhead.

100% Transparency

We believe our community deserves to know exactly how and why we operate. AcreetionOS is a father-daughter team: Darren and Natalie. We are both on Social Security Disability Income (SSDI). This is not a secret, and it directly affects how we structure this project.

Accepting monetary donations would create what the Social Security Administration calls "Substantial Gainful Activity" (SGA). Even small amounts of unreported income can jeopardize the healthcare and benefits that keep us alive. We are not willing to risk our livelihoods — or hide anything from the government — just to collect a few dollars.

The Real Cost of "Free" Money

Donations sound simple, but they come with hidden burdens:

  • Tax complexity: Even a $50 donation requires tracking, reporting, and potential tax liability.
  • Legal structure: To accept money safely, we'd need to form an LLC or non-profit — paying state fees, filing annual reports, and hiring accountants.
  • Time drain: Every hour spent on bookkeeping is an hour not spent improving AcreetionOS.
  • Benefit risk: As SSDI recipients, any unreported income could trigger overpayment demands, penalties, or loss of medical coverage.

Think Smarter, Not Harder

We learned early that duplicating effort is wasteful. The same principle applies here: instead of building a donation infrastructure that two people cannot sustainably manage, we focus on what we do best — writing code and supporting our users.

Other open-source projects have run into burnout, tax audits, and community drama over mishandled funds. With only two of us, we simply cannot afford to become another cautionary tale. We would rather be honest about our limitations than make promises we cannot keep.

What We Actually Need: Partnerships

Money is not our bottleneck. Sustainable infrastructure is. If you want to support AcreetionOS, here is what genuinely helps:

  • Mirror hosting: A VPS or bare-metal sponsor to host ISOs and packages reduces our out-of-pocket server costs.
  • Hardware donations: Test machines, routers, or spare drives help us verify compatibility.
  • Bandwidth partnerships: Colocation or CDN sponsorship gets our images to users faster.
  • Developer time: Contributing code, documentation, or bug reports is worth more than any PayPal transfer.

Want to Help?

If you represent an organization that can offer infrastructure, reach out directly. We are happy to discuss partnerships that keep this project alive without putting our benefits at risk.
