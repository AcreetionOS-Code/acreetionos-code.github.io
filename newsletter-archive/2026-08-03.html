August 03, 2026

Daily AcreetionOS Update - August 03, 2026

Our CI pipeline has been refined to publish the latest ISO directly to the public ISO server over SSH without unnecessary checkouts. This change reduces build time and ensures that the image you download is always the most recent stable version. The underlying GitLab CI now uses GIT_STRATEGY: none for the publish:iso job, eliminating redundant repository clones.

In addition to the CI tweaks, an initial commit was made to the acreetionos-code/acreetionos.gitlab.io repository, laying the groundwork for improved documentation hosting. Natalie and Darren continue to synchronize their efforts across GitHub and GitLab, keeping the build system tight and reliable. These behind‑the‑scenes updates keep the distribution flowing smoothly for everyone.

Upstream, the Linux kernel prepatch 7.2-rc6 has appeared in the testing branches, offering early access to new drivers and security patches. Meanwhile, Arch Linux users are discussing the first ISO powered by the 7.1 kernel, which aligns well with our goal of staying close to Arch’s rolling releases. We are monitoring these developments to decide the optimal moment to integrate newer kernel stacks into AcreetionOS.

Community news is buzzing after BetaNews featured AcreetionOS as an easy‑to‑use alternative to Windows 10/11, highlighting its privacy‑focused design and Cinnamon desktop. Readers praised the straightforward installation and the lack of telemetry, noting that it feels familiar yet refreshingly lightweight. The article has sparked a wave of interest on social media, with many newcomers joining our Discord to ask questions.

On our forums and mailing list, longtime Arch users have shared their experiences switching to AcreetionOS, often citing the reduced maintenance burden while still enjoying the Arch ecosystem. Several contributors have offered to help test the upcoming ISO on a variety of hardware, from older laptops to modern workstations. Their feedback is invaluable as we prepare the next release.

We are also calling for community members to assist with localization efforts, especially for the Cinnamon settings and documentation. If you have language skills and want to see AcreetionOS accessible to a wider audience, please reach out via our GitLab issue tracker. Your contributions will help make the distribution truly global.

For tips, remember that the Cinnamon desktop can be customized with themes and applets directly from the Settings menu, allowing you to tailor the look and feel without installing extra packages. The built‑in privacy tools, such as the firewall UI and the Timeshift snapshot manager, are enabled by default to keep your system secure. Spend a few minutes exploring these features to get the most out of your out‑of‑the‑box experience.

When installing AcreetionOS, the Calamares installer guides you through partitioning, user creation, and optional encryption steps with clear explanations at each screen. If you encounter any hiccups, the installer logs are saved to /var/log/calamares for troubleshooting, and our support channel is ready to help. Taking a quick glance at the summary screen before clicking “Install” can prevent common missteps.

Finally, stay connected with the project by joining our Discord server, following us on X and Bluesky, and subscribing to the newsletter for weekly highlights. Reporting bugs or suggesting features is as easy as opening an issue on GitHub, and every contribution helps shape the future of AcreetionOS. Thank you for being part of this community‑driven journey.
